## Chapter 23: Heijunka: Leveling Volume and Mix ✦

### The engineering question

*Is the variability we're trying to control with stock coming from the customer, or from us?*

A substantial share of the variability factories attribute to the market is generated internally — by lot-sizing policy, month-end closing, sales campaigns, and the "produce everything of one part number before switching" rule. Heijunka is the set of practices that attacks that share.

### The Two Levelings

**Volume leveling.** Producing the same total quantity every day, instead of chasing the peaks and valleys of orders. It absorbs variation with a small finished-goods stock instead of propagating it through the whole chain.

**Mix leveling.** Spreading part numbers throughout the day instead of grouping them into large batches.

Consider a month with demand of 600 A, 300 B, and 100 C (1,000 units, 20 days):

| Policy | Daily sequence | Response lead time for C | Stock needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monthly lot by part number | 12 days A, 6 days B, 2 days C | up to 18 days | very high |
| Daily lot | 30 A, 15 B, 5 C | 1 day | medium |
| Leveled (short EPEI) | AABAABAABAC repeated | < 1 shift | low |

The leveled pattern requires more changeovers. **That's why heijunka and SMED are inseparable:** without cutting setup time, leveling the mix destroys available capacity.

**EPEI (*Every Part Every Interval*)** is this piece's metric: how often can we produce each part number? "EPE day" means every part number gets produced every day. Cutting the EPEI is SMED's direct goal.

### The Leveling Box

The physical tool is the *heijunka box*: a grid with time-interval columns (say, every 20 minutes) and rows by part number. Kanbans are placed in the cells according to the leveled pattern, and the material handler pulls them in clock order (the *pitch*).

$$\text{Pitch} = \text{Takt time} \times \text{Quantity per container}$$

The pitch is the leveled factory's heartbeat: how often someone goes to the box to pull the next instruction. If the pitch is 20 minutes, the factory knows every 20 minutes whether it's ahead or behind — instead of finding out at the end of the shift.

### Worked example: build the leveled sequence and measure the effect

```py-r
--- python
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
from math import gcd
from functools import reduce

# --- Procura mensal por referência ---
procura = {"A": 600, "B": 300, "C": 100}
dias_uteis = 20
tempo_disp_dia_s = 27000
setup_s = 300                    # tempo de troca por mudança de referência

total_dia = sum(procura.values()) / dias_uteis
takt = tempo_disp_dia_s / total_dia
print(f"Procura diária total = {total_dia:.0f} peças | Takt = {takt:.1f} s/peça\n")

# --- Construir o padrão nivelado mínimo repetível ---
qtd_dia = {k: v / dias_uteis for k, v in procura.items()}
d = reduce(gcd, [int(v) for v in qtd_dia.values()])
padrao_base = {k: int(v) // d for k, v in qtd_dia.items()}
print("Quantidade diária:", {k: round(v) for k, v in qtd_dia.items()})
print("Padrão mínimo repetível:", padrao_base, f"(repetido {d}x por dia)\n")

# Intercalar o padrão o mais uniformemente possível (algoritmo de Bresenham/erro difundido)
def sequencia_nivelada(padrao):
    total = sum(padrao.values())
    restante = dict(padrao); credito = {k: 0.0 for k in padrao}
    seq = []
    for _ in range(total):
        for k in padrao:
            credito[k] += padrao[k] / total
        cand = {k: credito[k] for k in padrao if restante[k] > 0}
        escolhido = max(cand, key=cand.get)
        seq.append(escolhido); restante[escolhido] -= 1; credito[escolhido] -= 1
    return seq

seq = sequencia_nivelada(padrao_base)
print("Sequência nivelada (1 ciclo):", "".join(seq))
print("Sequência do dia inteiro    :", "".join(seq * d)[:60], "...\n")

# --- Comparar políticas ---
def avaliar(sequencia_dia, nome):
    trocas = sum(1 for i in range(1, len(sequencia_dia))
                 if sequencia_dia[i] != sequencia_dia[i-1])
    tempo_setup = trocas * setup_s
    # espera máxima até a referência mais rara ser produzida
    posicoes_C = [i for i, x in enumerate(sequencia_dia) if x == "C"]
    espera_max = (max(np.diff([-1] + posicoes_C)) if posicoes_C else len(sequencia_dia))
    return {"Política": nome, "Trocas/dia": trocas,
            "Tempo em setup (min)": round(tempo_setup / 60, 1),
            "% do tempo disponível": f"{tempo_setup/tempo_disp_dia_s:.1%}",
            "Máx. peças entre 2 C": espera_max}

lote = ["A"] * 30 + ["B"] * 15 + ["C"] * 5
nivelado = seq * d

print(pd.DataFrame([avaliar(lote, "Lote diário (A,B,C)"),
                    avaliar(nivelado, "Nivelado (heijunka)")]).to_string(index=False))

print(f"\nCom setup de {setup_s} s, nivelar custa "
      f"{(avaliar(nivelado,'')['Trocas/dia'] - avaliar(lote,'')['Trocas/dia']) * setup_s / 60:.0f} "
      f"min/dia adicionais de setup.")
setup_alvo = 60
print(f"Se o SMED reduzir o setup para {setup_alvo} s, esse custo cai para "
      f"{(avaliar(nivelado,'')['Trocas/dia'] - avaliar(lote,'')['Trocas/dia']) * setup_alvo / 60:.0f} min/dia.")
```

**Engineering reading.** The example makes explicit the trade-off almost never quantified in leveling discussions: **leveling the mix buys response capacity and pays for it in setup time**. As long as setup is expensive, the factory has a legitimate argument against leveling.

That's why the correct implementation sequence is SMED → heijunka → tight kanban, not the reverse. Trying to level first produces justified resistance from the shop floor.

### Common mistakes

1. **Leveling production without leveling internal demand.** If sales keeps concentrating orders at month-end, the leveled factory just piles up backlog. Heijunka is a company decision, not a production one.
2. **Leveling before SMED.** See above.
3. **Leveling part numbers with genuinely sporadic demand.** Long-tail part numbers rarely belong in the pattern; handle them make-to-order.
4. **Confusing leveling with forecasting.** Heijunka doesn't forecast better; it absorbs variation with a known buffer and stabilizes everything upstream.
5. **A leveling box with no pitch.** Without someone pulling cards at the defined rhythm, the box is decoration.
