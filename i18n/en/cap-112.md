## Chapter 112: AI in Quality and Its Governance: ISO/IEC 42001 and ISO/IEC 23894
### Practical addendum: validating and monitoring AI for quality

A model that looks accurate on the historical dataset can fail in production due to a change in product, tooling, supplier, operator, lighting, mix, process or logging policy. Before automating a quality decision, define: the use case; the risk of false accept versus false reject; the current SPC/rule-based baseline; the temporal split for train/validation/test; data traceability; the acceptance criterion; the accountable human; the rollback plan; and the limits of use.

In operation, monitor performance by product/shift/line, input and concept drift, exception rates, calibration, bias, latency and customer impact. Changes to the model, the data, or the threshold require change management and revalidation proportional to the risk.

### The engineering question

*Who is accountable when the model gets it wrong?*

This question has no technical answer. It has a management-system answer — and that is what the AI standards are here to standardize.

> **Note on the state of the standards (verified August 2026):**
> - **ISO/IEC 42001:2023** — *Information technology — Artificial intelligence — Management system*. 1st edition, published December 2023, in force and under systematic review. There is a European adoption (**EN ISO/IEC 42001**) published in 2026, relevant for anyone operating in the EU.
> - **ISO/IEC 23894:2023** — *Information technology — Artificial intelligence — Guidance on risk management*. 1st edition, published February 2023.
>
> Always confirm the current edition at iso.org before citing requirements in an official document.

### ISO/IEC 42001: a management system, not a technical manual

42001 is a **certifiable management-system standard**, built on the same harmonized structure as ISO 9001 and ISO 27001 (context, leadership, planning, support, operation, performance evaluation, improvement). Anyone who knows ISO 9001 will recognize the architecture immediately.

What it adds beyond ISO 9001:

| Element | Content |
|---|---|
| **AI system impact assessment** | Effects on people, groups and society — not just on the organization |
| **AI system life cycle** | Requirements, data, development, verification, deployment, operation, retirement |
| **Data management** | Provenance, quality, preparation, bias |
| **Transparency and information** | What is communicated to users and to those affected |
| **Human oversight** | Who can intervene, when, and how |
| **Roles** | Distinguishes AI provider, producer, user and subject |

**The connection to ISO 9001 is direct and worth exploiting:** an already-certified organization has most of the skeleton in place — policy, competence, documented information, internal audit, management review, corrective actions. What's missing is AI-specific: impact assessment, model life-cycle management, and human oversight.

### ISO/IEC 23894: AI risk

It follows the ISO 31000 structure (identification → analysis → evaluation → treatment → monitoring) and adds AI-specific risk categories: bias and fairness, robustness, security, privacy, transparency, explainability, human oversight, environmental impact and accountability.

### Applying it to a concrete case: a visual-inspection model

The table below translates the normative requirements into concrete quality-engineering actions — which is what's missing from most discussions of responsible AI.

| Risk area | Concrete question | Evidence to produce |
|---|---|---|
| **Bias** | Does the model perform worse on some part number, color, shift or line? | Metrics **broken down by subgroup**, not just global ones |
| **Robustness** | What happens with different lighting, a dirty lens, a new part? | Documented adversarial tests |
| **Explainability** | Can it justify a rejection to the customer? | Activation maps, examples, appeal rules |
| **Human oversight** | Who can reverse a model decision, and how? | Written procedure, with a log of reversals |
| **Monitoring** | How is degradation detected? | Performance control chart + reaction plan |
| **Data** | Where did the training images come from? Are they labeled correctly? | Lineage + **labeler agreement study** |
| **Accountability** | Who owns the model? | Named role, on the org chart |
| **Life cycle** | When is it retrained? When is it retired? | Criteria defined **before** deployment |

> **The row about training data deserves emphasis.** An inspection model is trained on labels produced by human inspectors. If those inspectors have an agreement Kappa of 0.6, the model is learning to replicate a mediocre measurement system — and its performance ceiling is the label's ceiling. **Attribute agreement analysis (PART VI) should precede the AI project**, not follow it.

```py-r
--- python
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd

rng = np.random.default_rng(9)

# ---------- Auditoria de equidade: desempenho DESAGREGADO ----------
n = 6000
d = pd.DataFrame({
    "referencia": rng.choice(["REF-A","REF-B","REF-C"], n, p=[.55,.35,.10]),
    "turno":      rng.choice(["M","T","N"], n, p=[.4,.35,.25]),
    "linha":      rng.choice(["L1","L2"], n, p=[.6,.4]),
})
d["defeito_real"] = rng.binomial(1, 0.05, n)

# O modelo é bom em geral, mas MUITO pior na referência C e no turno da noite
def deteta(r):
    base = 0.94
    if r.referencia == "REF-C": base -= 0.30      # pouca representação no treino
    if r.turno == "N":          base -= 0.12      # iluminação diferente
    return base
p_det = d.apply(deteta, axis=1)
d["modelo_deteta"] = np.where(d.defeito_real == 1,
                              rng.binomial(1, p_det),
                              rng.binomial(1, 0.02, n))   # falsos positivos

def metricas(g):
    tp = int(((g.modelo_deteta==1)&(g.defeito_real==1)).sum())
    fp = int(((g.modelo_deteta==1)&(g.defeito_real==0)).sum())
    fn = int(((g.modelo_deteta==0)&(g.defeito_real==1)).sum())
    return pd.Series({
        "n": len(g), "defeitos": int(g.defeito_real.sum()),
        "Revocação": round(tp/(tp+fn), 3) if tp+fn else np.nan,
        "Precisão":  round(tp/(tp+fp), 3) if tp+fp else np.nan,
        "Escapes": fn,
    })

print("DESEMPENHO GLOBAL")
print(metricas(d).to_frame("valor").T.to_string(index=False))

print("\n\nAUDITORIA DE EQUIDADE — desempenho DESAGREGADO")
for dim in ["referencia", "turno", "linha"]:
    print(f"\n   Por {dim}:")
    r = d.groupby(dim).apply(metricas, include_groups=False)
    print("   " + r.to_string().replace("\n", "\n   "))
    pior, melhor = r["Revocação"].min(), r["Revocação"].max()
    disparidade = melhor - pior
    print(f"   Disparidade máxima de revocação: {disparidade:.3f} "
          f"({'aceitável' if disparidade < 0.08 else 'REQUER AÇÃO'})")

print("\n\nCONCLUSÃO DA AUDITORIA")
print("   A revocação global esconde duas disparidades materiais:")
print("   a REF-C e o turno da noite têm desempenho muito inferior.")
print("   Um relatório que apresentasse apenas a métrica global teria")
print("   aprovado este modelo para produção.")
print("\n   Ações requeridas pela ISO/IEC 42001:")
print("     • investigar a causa (representatividade do treino; condições de iluminação)")
print("     • aumentar imagens de REF-C e de turno N no conjunto de treino")
print("     • manter inspeção humana nesses subgrupos até demonstração de paridade")
print("     • registar a limitação conhecida na documentação do sistema de IA")

# ---------- Ficha do sistema de IA (model card) ----------
ficha = {
    "sistema": "Inspeção visual automática — soldadura",
    "versao_modelo": "2.4.1",
    "proprietario": "Engenheiro da Qualidade — Área de Soldadura",
    "finalidade": "Deteção de porosidade e falta de penetração em cordão de soldadura",
    "papel_iso42001": "Utilizador de IA (modelo adquirido a fornecedor)",
    "dados_treino": "48.320 imagens, jan/2025 a mar/2026, linhas L1 e L2",
    "rotulagem": "3 inspetores certificados; Kappa de Fleiss = 0,81 (estudo AAA de 2025-11)",
    "limitacoes_conhecidas": [
        "desempenho inferior em REF-C (representação de 4% no treino)",
        "desempenho inferior em turno N (iluminação distinta)",
        "não validado para referências introduzidas após 2026-03",
    ],
    "supervisao_humana": "Toda a rejeição é confirmada por operador antes do descarte; "
                         "amostragem de 2% das aprovações revista diariamente",
    "monitorizacao": "Carta p semanal da taxa de deteção sobre peças-padrão; "
                     "revisão trimestral de calibração",
    "criterio_retreino": "revocação abaixo de 0,90 em duas semanas consecutivas, "
                         "ou introdução de nova referência",
```

### What changes in the quality engineer's role

AI doesn't take work away from the quality engineer — it shifts it. The skills that gain value:

1. **Validating measurement systems that happen to be models.** The whole MSA apparatus applies, and whoever masters it has an immediate advantage.
2. **Designing human oversight.** Where the person steps in, with what authority, and with what record.
3. **Defining retirement criteria.** The question almost never asked at deployment time: *under what conditions do we turn this off?* Defining it before deployment is far easier than doing so afterward.
4. **Auditing fairness and robustness.** This is stratified analysis — a classic quality competency applied to a new object.
5. **Managing change.** A model in production is a process change, and goes through the same change-management requirements: impact analysis, validation, approval, communication, training.

> **AI does not replace human judgment in quality.** It replaces repetitive detection tasks — and shifts human work toward decision-making, validation and accountability. Which raises, rather than lowers, the bar for the competence of whoever decides.

### Common mistakes

1. **Treating AI as an IT project.** It's a change to the production process and to the management system.
2. **Reporting only global metrics.** Always break down by part number, shift, line and period.
3. **Not studying the reliability of the training labels.** The model cannot be better than the standard it was taught against.
4. **Deploying without a contingency plan.** If the system fails, what happens in the next two hours?
5. **Not defining retirement criteria.** Without them, degraded models stay in production indefinitely, because turning them off requires a decision nobody wants to make.
6. **Confusing ISO/IEC 42001 certification with a guarantee that the model works.** The standard certifies the AI **management system**, not the performance of a specific model — just as ISO 9001 does not certify that the product is good.

### 2026 update — Responsible AI

**ISO/IEC 42001:2023** remains in force in 2026 as the AI management-system standard. **ISO/IEC 23894:2023** remains the guidance reference for AI risk management.

The quality engineer must separate three things: (1) the model's technical performance; (2) the performance and risk of the socio-technical system; (3) the effectiveness of the management system. ISO/IEC 42001 does not certify that a specific model is "correct" or error-free.

For systems that influence quality decisions, add to the validation cycle: label quality/agreement, stratified performance, drift, retirement criteria, human oversight, security, privacy, version traceability and impact assessment.
