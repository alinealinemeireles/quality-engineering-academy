## Chapter 25: TPM and OEE: Total Productive Maintenance
### The engineering question

*When the machine stops, is that a maintenance problem or a quality problem?*

TPM's answer is: neither one in isolation. Stoppages, micro-stops, and speed losses degrade process stability, and an unstable process has no capability. TPM is the bridge between equipment availability and statistical process capability.

### OEE: the metric and its traps

$$\text{OEE} = \text{Availability} \times \text{Performance} \times \text{Quality}$$

| Component | Formula | Losses it captures |
|---|---|---|
| **Availability** | Operating time ÷ Planned time | Breakdowns, setups, and adjustments |
| **Performance** | (Ideal cycle time × Parts produced) ÷ Operating time | Micro-stops, speed reduction |
| **Quality** | Good parts ÷ Parts produced | Scrap, rework, startup losses |

These are TPM's **six big losses**, two per component.

**OEE's three traps:**

1. **Planned time is negotiable, and that's where the number gets corrupted.** Excluding "planned" stoppages from the denominator that are actually the consequence of problems (extraordinary cleanups, recurring material waits) produces 90% OEEs at factories with serious problems.
2. **OEE isn't comparable across equipment.** A 65% OEE on bottleneck equipment is worth far more than 92% on equipment with slack. **Improving the OEE of a non-bottleneck doesn't produce a single extra part** — that's the Theory of Constraints intersecting lean.
3. **High OEE can be overproduction.** If the metric is occupancy, the shop floor's rational response is to produce for stock.

> Use OEE as a **bottleneck loss-diagnosis tool**, not as a people-performance KPI.

### The Pillars of TPM

Of the eight classic pillars, two produce most of the result:

**Autonomous maintenance.** The operator takes over cleaning, lubrication, tightening, and daily inspection. The logic isn't saving maintenance hours: it's that **cleaning is inspecting**. The operator who cleans detects the leak, the play, and the vibration weeks before the breakdown.

**Planned maintenance.** The maintenance team frees itself from corrective work and moves to condition-based preventive maintenance and reliability improvements (MTBF, root-cause analysis of recurring breakdowns).

The rest: focused improvement (kobetsu kaizen), early equipment management, quality maintenance, training, administrative TPM, safety, and environment.

**Link to reliability.** MTBF, MTTR, and availability are reliability quantities — see PART XI. The basic relationship:

$$\text{Inherent availability} = \frac{\text{MTBF}}{\text{MTBF} + \text{MTTR}}$$

This shows there are two paths to availability: fail less often (MTBF) or repair faster (MTTR). Autonomous TPM attacks the first; maintenance organization and spare parts attack the second.

### Worked example: decomposing OEE and choosing where to attack

```py-r
--- python
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np

# --- Dados de um turno no equipamento gargalo ---
tempo_calendario_min   = 480
paragens_planeadas_min = 30     # pausas e reunião de turno
avarias_min            = 42
setup_min              = 55
microparagens_min      = 28
ct_ideal_s             = 12.0
pecas_produzidas       = 1_450
pecas_refugo           = 38
pecas_retrabalho       = 26

tempo_planeado = tempo_calendario_min - paragens_planeadas_min
tempo_operacao = tempo_planeado - avarias_min - setup_min

disponibilidade = tempo_operacao / tempo_planeado
desempenho = (ct_ideal_s * pecas_produzidas / 60) / tempo_operacao
qualidade = (pecas_produzidas - pecas_refugo - pecas_retrabalho) / pecas_produzidas
oee = disponibilidade * desempenho * qualidade

print(f"Tempo planeado    : {tempo_planeado} min")
print(f"Tempo de operação : {tempo_operacao} min\n")
print(f"Disponibilidade : {disponibilidade:6.1%}   (avarias {avarias_min} min + setup {setup_min} min)")
print(f"Desempenho      : {desempenho:6.1%}")
print(f"Qualidade       : {qualidade:6.1%}   (refugo {pecas_refugo} + retrabalho {pecas_retrabalho})")
print(f"OEE             : {oee:6.1%}\n")

# --- Traduzir cada perda em PEÇAS, que é a moeda da decisão ---
cap_teorica = tempo_planeado * 60 / ct_ideal_s
perdas = pd.DataFrame([
    ("Avarias",        avarias_min * 60 / ct_ideal_s,  "Manutenção planeada / análise de causa"),
    ("Setup e ajuste", setup_min   * 60 / ct_ideal_s,  "SMED"),
    ("Microparagens e velocidade",
        cap_teorica * disponibilidade * (1 - desempenho), "Manutenção autónoma / condições básicas"),
    ("Refugo e retrabalho",
        cap_teorica * disponibilidade * desempenho * (1 - qualidade), "SPC / MSA / FMEA"),
], columns=["Perda", "Peças perdidas", "Contramedida lean"])
perdas["Peças perdidas"] = perdas["Peças perdidas"].round(0)
perdas["% da capacidade teórica"] = (perdas["Peças perdidas"] / cap_teorica * 100).round(1)
perdas = perdas.sort_values("Peças perdidas", ascending=False)

print(f"Capacidade teórica do turno: {cap_teorica:.0f} peças")
print(f"Produção boa real          : {pecas_produzidas - pecas_refugo - pecas_retrabalho:.0f} peças\n")
print(perdas.to_string(index=False))

top = perdas.iloc[0]
print(f"\n=> Maior perda: {top['Perda']} ({top['Peças perdidas']:.0f} peças/turno). "
      f"Contramedida: {top['Contramedida lean']}.")

# --- Disponibilidade inerente a partir de MTBF/MTTR ---
n_avarias, mttr_min = 3, 14
mtbf = tempo_operacao / n_avarias
print(f"\nMTBF = {mtbf:.0f} min | MTTR = {mttr_min} min | "
      f"Disponibilidade inerente = {mtbf/(mtbf+mttr_min):.1%}")
print(f"Se o MTBF duplicar (mesma MTTR): {2*mtbf/(2*mtbf+mttr_min):.1%}")
print(f"Se a MTTR for para metade (mesmo MTBF): {mtbf/(mtbf+mttr_min/2):.1%}")
```

**Engineering reading.** Converting the losses from percentage into **parts** is the step that makes OEE actionable. Percentages of different components don't add up or compare intuitively; parts lost per shift add up and can be prioritized by Pareto.

The final table also builds the bridge to the rest of the manual: every loss has a countermeasure with its own chapter. Setup → SMED. Micro-stops → autonomous maintenance. Scrap → SPC, MSA, and FMEA. OEE doesn't fix anything; it tells you **which chapter to go to next**.

### Common mistakes

1. **Chasing OEE on non-bottlenecks.** Consumes resources and doesn't increase output.
2. **Manipulating planned time.** Classifying problems as planned stoppage is the most common way to manufacture good-looking OEEs.
3. **Using the "vendor nominal" cycle time as ideal.** If the machine has never hit that cycle with this product and this material, the performance component stays permanently unrealistic.
4. **Autonomous maintenance with no training or allocated time.** It turns into task-shifting, not TPM, and fails within a few months.
5. **Ignoring rework in the quality component.** A reworked part isn't a first-pass good part; including it inflates the number and hides the cost.
