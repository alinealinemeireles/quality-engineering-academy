## Chapter 20: Takt Time, Cycle Time, Lead Time, and Little's Law ✦

### The engineering question

*How many of these four different times is management calling "production time"?*

Much of the unproductive discussion about operational performance gets resolved with vocabulary discipline. These four times answer different questions and aren't interchangeable.

### The definitions, with no ambiguity

| Time | Definition | Who sets it | Unit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Takt time** | Available production time ÷ customer demand over the same period | **The customer** | time / part |
| **Cycle time (CT)** | Time between two consecutive parts leaving the same station | The process | time / part |
| **Lead time (LT)** | Total time from order/material entry to delivery | The whole system | time |
| **Value-added time (VA)** | The fraction of lead time during which the product is being transformed in a way the customer would pay for | The process design | time |

$$\text{Takt} = \frac{\text{Available time in the period}}{\text{Customer demand in the period}}$$

**Available time** means net time: planned breaks, shift meetings, and planned maintenance subtracted out. Breakdowns and unplanned setups aren't subtracted — those are problems to solve, not to hide in the takt's denominator.

**The target relationship:** $CT \leq \text{Takt}$ across every operation, with the bottleneck as close to takt as possible without exceeding it. $CT$ far below takt means overproduction or installed overcapacity.

### Little's Law

Little's Law is queuing theory's most useful and least-used result in industry:

$$\text{WIP} = \text{Throughput rate} \times \text{Lead time} \qquad \Longleftrightarrow \qquad \text{Lead time} = \frac{\text{WIP}}{\text{Throughput rate}}$$

It holds for any stable system, with no assumptions about the distribution of times. The practical consequences are strong:

1. **To cut lead time without investing, cut WIP.** If the throughput rate is capped by the bottleneck and doesn't change, cutting WIP in half cuts lead time in half. It's the arithmetic argument for the pull system.
2. **Adding WIP never speeds up delivery.** It only increases how long each part spends in queue — and how long it takes to detect a defect.
3. **Long lead time hides quality problems.** With 3 days of WIP between the operation that generates a defect and the one that detects it, 3 days of scrap get built before anyone finds out.

> This third consequence is why lean and quality aren't separate programs. **Reducing WIP is a quality action**, because it shortens the defect's feedback loop.

### Worked example: where is this line's lead time?

```py-r
--- python
import pandas as pd

# --- Dados da linha ---
turnos_dia = 2
horas_turno = 8
pausas_min_turno = 45
procura_dia = 720                      # peças/dia

tempo_disp_dia_s = turnos_dia * (horas_turno * 3600 - pausas_min_turno * 60)
takt = tempo_disp_dia_s / procura_dia
print(f"Tempo disponível = {tempo_disp_dia_s:,.0f} s/dia")
print(f"Takt time        = {takt:.1f} s/peça\n")

etapas = pd.DataFrame({
    "Etapa":      ["Corte", "Fila 1", "Maquinação", "Fila 2", "Tratamento",
                   "Fila 3", "Montagem", "Inspeção final"],
    "Tipo":       ["VA", "Espera", "VA", "Espera", "VA", "Espera", "VA", "NVA-necessário"],
    "Tempo_s":    [55, 14400, 68, 28800, 40, 7200, 62, 35],
})

total = etapas["Tempo_s"].sum()
va = etapas.loc[etapas["Tipo"] == "VA", "Tempo_s"].sum()
espera = etapas.loc[etapas["Tipo"] == "Espera", "Tempo_s"].sum()

etapas["% do lead time"] = (etapas["Tempo_s"] / total * 100).round(2)
print(etapas.to_string(index=False))

print(f"\nLead time total        = {total/3600:.2f} h")
print(f"Tempo de valor acrescentado = {va:.0f} s ({va/total:.2%} do lead time)")
print(f"Tempo em fila          = {espera/3600:.2f} h ({espera/total:.1%})")
print(f"Rácio de eficiência de ciclo (PCE) = {va/total:.2%}")

# --- Lei de Little: quanto WIP existe implicitamente? ---
taxa_saida_por_s = procura_dia / tempo_disp_dia_s     # peças por segundo
wip = taxa_saida_por_s * total
print(f"\nLei de Little: WIP implícito = {wip:.0f} peças em curso")

# E se cortarmos as filas a um terço?
total_reduzido = va + etapas.loc[etapas['Tipo']=='NVA-necessário','Tempo_s'].sum() + espera/3
print(f"Se o WIP em fila cair para 1/3: lead time = {total_reduzido/3600:.2f} h "
      f"({(1-total_reduzido/total):.0%} de redução)")
```

```py-r
--- python
%%R
turnos_dia <- 2; horas_turno <- 8; pausas_min <- 45; procura_dia <- 720
tempo_disp_dia_s <- turnos_dia * (horas_turno * 3600 - pausas_min * 60)
takt <- tempo_disp_dia_s / procura_dia

etapas <- data.frame(
  Etapa   = c("Corte","Fila 1","Maquinacao","Fila 2","Tratamento","Fila 3","Montagem","Inspecao"),
  Tipo    = c("VA","Espera","VA","Espera","VA","Espera","VA","NVA-necessario"),
  Tempo_s = c(55, 14400, 68, 28800, 40, 7200, 62, 35)
)
total  <- sum(etapas$Tempo_s)
va     <- sum(etapas$Tempo_s[etapas$Tipo == "VA"])
espera <- sum(etapas$Tempo_s[etapas$Tipo == "Espera"])

cat(sprintf("Takt = %.1f s/peca\n", takt))
cat(sprintf("Lead time = %.2f h | VA = %.0f s (%.2f%%) | Fila = %.1f%%\n",
            total/3600, va, 100*va/total, 100*espera/total))
cat(sprintf("Lei de Little: WIP = %.0f pecas\n", (procura_dia/tempo_disp_dia_s) * total))
```

**Engineering reading.** The transformation time is 225 seconds; the lead time is nearly 14 hours. The process cycle efficiency (PCE) sits well below 1%, a perfectly typical value on a non-lean line, and one that tends to surprise management.

The practical consequence: **an improvement project attacking the 225 seconds of VA has a ceiling of 0.5% gain in lead time.** All the gain is in the queues. That's why a VSM's first question isn't "how do we speed up the machines?" but "why is there 8 hours of material sitting between machining and heat treatment?"

### Common mistakes

1. **Subtracting breakdowns from available time.** This artificially lowers the required takt and turns a reliability problem into a planning number. Breakdowns belong in OEE (see the TPM chapter), not in the takt's denominator.
2. **Calculating takt per line instead of per product family.** If the line runs three families with different demands, there are three takts — or heijunka is used to create a single rhythm.
3. **Comparing cycle time with lead time.** They're quantities of different scale; the relevant comparison is CT against takt.
4. **Assuming Little's Law requires special distributions.** It doesn't. It only requires the system to be stable over the observed period (what comes in, goes out).
5. **Measuring PCE and reporting it as a people-productivity indicator.** PCE measures the flow's design, not the effort of the people working it.
