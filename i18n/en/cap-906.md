## Chapter 906: Structured Problem Solving — 5 Whys, 8D and CAPA

### The engineering question

*The 8D was filled out, signed, and filed. Three weeks later, the same defect shows up on a
different part, from the same process. What was missing?*

Almost always, the answer lies in the difference between **correction** and **corrective action**.
Correction swaps the bad part for a good one — it solves today's problem. Corrective action
changes the process that produced the bad part — it prevents tomorrow's problem. A well-filled-out
report that contains only corrections is a complete document about a problem that's still coming
back.

## 2 · Quick fix vs. root cause: the same problem, two treatments

```plotly
capa-recorrencia
```

The chart shows the same defect handled two ways in comparable organizations. **Quick fix**
(replace the part, adjust on the spot) keeps recurrence oscillating — the problem never
disappears, it's only postponed. **Root cause** (investigate why it happened and change the
process) reduces recurrence sustainably, but takes two to three months to reach zero — because the
real cause is almost never obvious on the first Why.

## 3 · Correction, corrective action, and preventive action aren't synonyms

| | Focus | Objective | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Correction** | The immediate effect | Restore operation now | Rework the part, replace the component |
| **Corrective action** | The cause of a problem that already occurred | Eliminate the cause and prevent recurrence | Revise the procedure, train, adjust a parameter |
| **Preventive action** | The cause of a problem that hasn't occurred yet | Eliminate the risk before it becomes a defect | Poka-yoke, FMEA update, new checklist |

> **The sentence that resolves the confusion.** Correcting solves today's problem. Corrective
> action prevents tomorrow's problem. A complete quality report has both — and doesn't mistake one
> for the other.

---

## 4 · The branching 5 Whys — because the cause is rarely a straight line

The most common mistake with the 5 Whys is treating it as a single chain. In practice, each "why"
can have more than one valid answer, and each branch needs to be checked at the gemba before
moving on:

| Level | Question | Answer found | Confirmed at the gemba? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Symptom | Product shipped with a dimensional defect | — | — |
| Why 1 | Why was it out of dimension? | Tool out of adjustment | ✔ measured on the machine |
| Why 2 | Why was the tool out of adjustment? | No check at the start of the shift | ✔ checklist record missing |
| Why 3 | Why wasn't there a check? | Operator didn't know it was mandatory | ✔ confirmed in interview |
| Why 4 | Why didn't they know? | Wasn't trained on the current procedure | ✔ training record shows nothing |
| Why 5 (root cause) | Why weren't they trained? | No training matrix exists for that station | ✔ matrix doesn't cover the station |

The root cause here isn't "operator made a mistake" — it's "there's no control that guarantees any
operator at that station gets trained." The right corrective action targets the training matrix,
not the individual.

## 5 · Where to investigate first

```plotly
capa-pareto
```

Before opening an 8D for each nonconformity in isolation, look at the period's Pareto: if
"material out of spec" and "setup error" already account for more than half the occurrences, the
root cause is probably systemic (supplier, specification, or setup standard) — it isn't worth
treating each occurrence as an isolated case.

## 6 · The full 8D

| Discipline | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| **D0 — Preparation** | Does this need a formal 8D, or does a simple correction solve it? |
| **D1 — Team** | Who has the knowledge and authority to investigate and act? |
| **D2 — Problem description** | What, where, when, how much — in facts, not opinions |
| **D3 — Containment** | What keeps the problem from reaching more customers right now? |
| **D4 — Root cause** | Why did it happen — and why wasn't it caught before it shipped? |
| **D5 — Proposed corrective actions** | What eliminates the root cause, with a deadline and an owner? |
| **D6 — Implementation and validation** | Was the action implemented, and did the indicator actually improve? |
| **D7 — Recurrence prevention** | Were the FMEA, control plan, and work instruction updated? |
| **D8 — Closure and recognition** | Are the lessons documented where someone will find them later? |

## 7 · Prioritizing with data: RPN and Pareto together

```py-r
--- python
import pandas as pd

# Simplified log of the month's nonconformances
ncr = pd.DataFrame({
    "categoria": ["Material out of spec", "Setup error", "Training gap",
                  "Procedure deviation", "Uncalibrated instrument", "Other"],
    "ocorrencias": [31, 24, 18, 13, 9, 5],
    "severidade": [8, 6, 5, 6, 7, 3],       # 1-10, impact if it reaches the customer
})

ncr["pct"] = ncr["ocorrencias"] / ncr["ocorrencias"].sum() * 100
ncr["pct_acum"] = ncr["pct"].cumsum()
ncr["indice_prioridade"] = ncr["ocorrencias"] * ncr["severidade"]   # frequency x severity

ncr_ordenado = ncr.sort_values("indice_prioridade", ascending=False)
print(ncr_ordenado[["categoria", "ocorrencias", "pct_acum", "indice_prioridade"]]
      .to_string(index=False))

foco_80 = ncr[ncr["pct_acum"] <= 80]["categoria"].tolist()
print(f"\nCategories that add up to the first 80% of occurrences: {foco_80}")
print("Investigate these before opening an individual 8D for each isolated case.")
--- r
ncr <- data.frame(
  categoria = c("Material out of spec", "Setup error", "Training gap",
                "Procedure deviation", "Uncalibrated instrument", "Other"),
  ocorrencias = c(31, 24, 18, 13, 9, 5),
  severidade = c(8, 6, 5, 6, 7, 3)
)

ncr$pct <- ncr$ocorrencias / sum(ncr$ocorrencias) * 100
ncr$pct_acum <- cumsum(ncr$pct)

ncr_ordenado <- ncr[order(-ncr$indice_prioridade), ]

foco_80 <- ncr$categoria[ncr$pct_acum <= 80]
cat("\nCategories that add up to the first 80% of occurrences:\n")
print(foco_80)
```

### Common mistakes

1. **Skipping containment and going straight to root cause.** While you investigate, the problem
   keeps shipping out the door.
2. **Treating the first "why" as the root cause.** Five levels is a heuristic, not a fixed rule —
   stop when the cause is actionable, not when you've counted to five.
3. **Closing the 8D without D6 (validation).** An implemented action isn't the same as an
   effective one.
4. **Confusing correction with corrective action in the report.** See section 3 — it's the most
   common and most expensive mistake.
5. **Opening an 8D for each isolated occurrence when the Pareto shows a systemic cause.** In that
   case, one well-targeted CAPA resolves it faster than ten parallel reports.
6. **Not updating the FMEA after the CAPA (D7).** If the failure mode wasn't in there, it'll show
   up again on another project.
