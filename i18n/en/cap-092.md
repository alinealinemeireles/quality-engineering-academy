## Chapter 92: ISO 19011:2026 — Management System Auditing

### ISO 19011:2026 — What It Is and Isn't

ISO 19011:2026 was published in May 2026 and replaced ISO 19011:2018. It's a **guide** for auditing management systems — principles, program, conduct, competence, and evaluation of auditors — not a certifiable standard. System certification and body accreditation follow their own rules; ISO 19011 should therefore never be presented as authorization to certify organizations.

The current edition strengthens the treatment of risk, technology, digitalization, and virtual environments. Remote auditing is only appropriate when method, information security, access to evidence, competence, and risk allow for sufficient evidence.

### A Process-, Risk-, and Evidence-Based Audit Method

1. Define objective, scope, criteria, team, independence, and confidentiality.
2. Plan risk-driven sampling: changes, performance, complaints, special characteristics, suppliers, and prior findings.
3. Follow process trails (input → activity → output → customer) and triangulate interview, observation, record, and data.
4. Write up the finding with requirement/criterion, verifiable evidence, observed condition, and impact — without prescribing a solution when the auditor's role doesn't allow it.
5. Assess the action plan and its effectiveness in proportion to risk; close only with evidence of implementation and effectiveness.

**Common pitfall:** a checklist is a memory aid; it doesn't replace professional judgment, well-founded sampling, or objective evidence.

### Competence and the Audit Program

Competence combines ethical behavior, knowledge of audit principles, the applicable standard, process/technology/sector, legal requirements, and the ability to communicate conclusions. The program should record risks, objectives, methods (on-site/remote/hybrid), resources, competence, results, and improvement of the program itself.

Status source: [ISO 19011:2026](https://www.iso.org/standard/19011). For IATF, regulated, or accreditation audits, supplement with the applicable sector-specific rules.

### 2026 Update — ISO 19011

**ISO 19011:2026**, 4th edition, was published in May 2026 and replaced ISO 19011:2018. The revision strengthens the application of auditing in digital and virtual environments and broadens attention to analyzing and treating risks and opportunities within the audit program.

The instructional flow should remain: audit program → planning → execution → evidence → conclusions → reporting → follow-up → program improvement. ISO 19011 provides **guidance**, not system certification requirements.
