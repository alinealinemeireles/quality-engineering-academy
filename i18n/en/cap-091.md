## Chapter 91: ISO 9001 in 2026

### ISO 9001:2026

**ISO 9001:2026** specifies the requirements for a quality management system and is the certifiable standard in the ISO 9000 family. ISO 9000:2026 is its normative reference for terms and definitions (Chapter 90).

Practitioners should always keep these separate: published-standard requirements; certification-body interpretation; customer-specific requirements; and internal good practice. This prevents an expectation or interpretation from being mistaken for an auditable obligation.

### Core Topics of ISO 9001:2026

| Topic | Where | What it means in practice |
|---|---|---|
| Core terms | Clause 3 (3.1 to 3.20) | Twenty essential terms (organization, interested party, risk, process, documented information, nonconformity, corrective action, audit, measurement, monitoring…) sit in the text itself; ISO 9000 is the normative reference for the rest of the vocabulary |
| Quality culture and ethical behavior | 5.1.1 i); 7.3 e); note to 7.1.4 | Top management must **promote** quality culture and ethical behavior; people must be **aware** of them; the environment for the operation of processes includes social and psychological factors that this culture influences |
| Risks and opportunities | 6.1.1, 6.1.2, 6.1.3 | Joint determination (6.1.1) and **distinct** actions: risks are determined, analyzed, and evaluated, with actions proportionate to their potential impact (6.1.2); opportunities get their own actions, appropriate to the context (6.1.3) |
| Opportunity-based thinking | 0.3.1; 5.1.1 k) | Alongside risk-based thinking, leadership must promote opportunity-based thinking |
| Change management | 6.3 a)–g); 5.3 f) | Planning of changes includes communicating the changes, monitoring and evaluating their effectiveness, and reviewing their results; the integrity of the QMS must be maintained while changes are planned and implemented |
| Climate change | 4.1; note to 4.2 | The organization must determine whether climate change is a relevant issue |
| Interested parties | 4.2 c) | Determine **which** interested-party requirements will be addressed through the QMS, and monitor and review that information |
| Effectiveness evaluation | 9.1.3 e)–f); 9.3.2 g)–h) | Data analysis and management review evaluate the effectiveness of actions on risks and on opportunities separately |
| Annex A | Annex A | Clarifies structure and terminology ("appropriate" vs. "applicable," "consider" vs. "take into account," "ensure," "continual") without adding requirements |

**Structure:** the harmonized ten-clause structure (requirements in clauses 4 to 10), the seven quality management principles, the process approach with the PDCA cycle, no requirement for a quality manual, and the documented-information logic. The notes include current examples — infrastructure for on-site, remote, or hybrid work (7.1.3), knowledge held in digital systems (7.1.6), social media as a source of customer-satisfaction information (9.1.2) — but notes aren't requirements.

### Application Roadmap by Clause

| ISO 9001:2026 Block | Engineering Question | Typical Objective Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 4. Context | Which issues (including climate), interested parties, and requirements influence QMS outcomes? | reviewed context analysis, decision on the relevance of climate, interested-party requirements addressed by the QMS, scope |
| 5. Leadership | Who's accountable for the system and the customer, and how does management promote quality culture and ethics? | policy, objectives, responsibilities, evidence of promoting quality culture and ethical behavior, management review |
| 6. Planning | How are risks and opportunities handled separately, and how are changes planned? | risk register (analysis, evaluation, and proportionate actions), opportunity portfolio, measurable targets, change plan with effectiveness evaluation |
| 7. Support | Are people, environment, competence, awareness, communication, knowledge, and documented information adequate? | competence matrix, calibration, awareness actions on quality culture and ethics, controlled documents |
| 8. Operation | How are requirements, design, suppliers, production, and release controlled? | contract, FMEA/control where applicable, traceability, inspection/release |
| 9. Evaluation | Is performance monitored and audited, and is the effectiveness of actions on risks and opportunities evaluated? | KPI, satisfaction, audit, data analysis, management review with inputs 9.3.2 g) and h) |
| 10. Improvement | Do nonconformities lead to effective action? | containment, root cause, CAPA, effectiveness verification, update of risks and opportunities |

In 4.1, the organization must determine whether climate change is a relevant issue, and the note to 4.2 reminds that interested parties can have requirements related to it. There's still no universal list of environmental controls within ISO 9001 — that's the domain of ISO 14001 (Chapter 90).

### Applied Exercise: From Nonconformity to Effectiveness

For a customer complaint about a wrong product delivery, produce: (a) containment and traceability of the lots; (b) root-cause analysis that tests hypotheses rather than blaming the operator; (c) correction, corrective action, and owner; (d) an effectiveness metric over a defined period; and (e) an update to risk, training, work instructions, the identification system, and auditing — and, if necessary, to the risks and opportunities determined during planning (10.2.1 e)). Tie the evidence back to clauses 8, 9, and 10, without confusing immediate correction with systemic corrective action.

Status source: [ISO 9001 — official page](https://www.iso.org/iso-9001-quality-management.html) (ISO 9001:2026).

### Implementation — Engineering Roadmap

1. **Diagnosis** clause by clause, paying attention to the topics in the table above: 4.1 (climate), 4.2 c), 5.1.1 i)–k), 6.1.2/6.1.3, 6.3, and 7.3 e).
2. **Handle risks and opportunities separately**: risks with analysis, evaluation, and action proportionate to impact; opportunities with their own action, owner, and effectiveness evaluation.
3. **Make quality culture observable**: expected behaviors, ease of reporting errors and near misses, leadership decisions that demonstrate it — not a poster with values.
4. **Structure change management** according to 6.3 a)–g), 8.1, and 8.5.6.
5. **Prepare internal audit and management review** with inputs 9.3.2 g) and h).
6. **Train internal auditors** on the standard and plan the certification audit with the certification body.

**Important:** sector standards built on ISO 9001 (IATF 16949, ISO 13485, AS9100) have their own requirements and scheme rules.
