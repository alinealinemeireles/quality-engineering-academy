## Chapter 59: ISO 10012:2026 — Measurement Management Systems

### Regulatory update: ISO 10012:2026

**ISO 10012:2026** was published in February 2026 and replaced ISO 10012:2003. It specifies requirements for a measurement management system that gives confidence in the validity and reliability of results used to demonstrate conformance and support decisions. It isn't synonymous with calibration, nor does it replace ISO/IEC 17025 laboratory competence.

For quality-engineering purposes, the central question is: **is the measurement result fit for its intended use and reliable enough for the decision?** Answering it requires treating equipment, method, person, environment, software, reference standard, metrological confirmation frequency, and the risk of a wrong decision as a system.

### How to apply it to the measurement system

Use the following evidence chain:

1. customer/design requirement/CTQ and tolerance;
2. adequate measurement method and resolution;
3. metrological traceability where applicable;
4. metrological confirmation, including calibration/verification and equipment status;
5. an MSA study proportional to risk — bias, linearity, stability, and GR&R by ANOVA where applicable;
6. rules for out-of-condition data, software/spreadsheets, and record retention;
7. response when the measurement isn't reliable: assess already-accepted/rejected product, contain, correct, and revalidate.

**Important distinction:** a valid calibration doesn't, on its own, prove the method is capable for a specific tolerance; MSA also doesn't replace traceability or metrological confirmation.

### Measurement audit checklist

- Is there an explicit link between each critical characteristic, its method, and the decision made?
- Is measurement uncertainty/error considered where it could change the conformance decision?
- Do standards and equipment have identification, condition, interval, and confirmation history?
- Do MSA studies represent real parts, operators, conditions, and operating range?
- Is there control over measurement software, spreadsheet formulas, and program changes?
- Does an out-of-condition instrument trigger a retrospective assessment of impact on product and data?

Status source: [ISO 10012:2026](https://www.iso.org/standard/10012). Consult the licensed standard for the complete requirements.

### 2026 update — engineering reading

**ISO 10012:2026**, published in February 2026, replaces ISO 10012:2003. The structure was reorganized to align with the harmonized structure of management-system standards. The goal remains ensuring confidence in the validity and reliability of measurement results.

For the quality engineer, the implication is to broaden the calibration discussion into **measurement process management**: measurement requirements, competence, resources, documented information, metrological confirmation, fitness for use, and the risk of an incorrect decision. ISO 10012 doesn't replace ISO/IEC 17025 for laboratory competence.

**2026 rule of thumb:** a current calibration demonstrates calibration status; on its own, it doesn't demonstrate that the complete measurement system is fit for its intended use.
