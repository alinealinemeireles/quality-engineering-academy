## Chapter 101: Automotive Quality — Core Tools, IATF, and Application
### Integrated Application of Automotive Quality

> This chapter presents an integrated application for study purposes. It **does not replace** AIAG, AIAG&VDA, IATF, or VDA manuals, or customer-specific requirements (CSRs), which prevail in case of conflict.

### 1. Automotive Architecture and Requirements Hierarchy

The correct chain starts with the customer's contract, drawing, and CSR; continues through IATF 16949, certification rules, and the Core Tools manuals; and ends at the local execution system. "Usual practice" doesn't override a CSR. As of 1/1/2025, **IATF Rules 6th Edition** are effective for recognized certification bodies; Rules 5th Edition and their corresponding SIs/FAQs have become obsolete. Source: [IATF Global Oversight](https://www.iatfglobaloversight.org/news/1-april-2024-rules-6th-publication/).

For study purposes, many practitioners call APQP, PPAP, FMEA, MSA, and SPC the five Core Tools. The **Control Plan** is a critical deliverable integrated into APQP/PFMEA; some materials present it separately as a sixth tool. Always state the convention you're adopting, rather than treating the count as a universal requirement.

### 2. APQP and PPAP: From Requirement to Production Evidence

| APQP Phase | Question That Needs Answering | Typical Outputs |
|---|---|---|
| Plan and define program | What do the customer, safety, legislation, and capability require? | voice of the customer, feasibility, objectives, timeline, risks |
| Product design and development | Is the product robust to requirements and risks? | DFMEA, drawings, special characteristics, DVP&R |
| Process design and development | Can the process realize the product repeatedly? | flow diagram, PFMEA, control plan, work instructions, MSA |
| Product/process validation | Does the actual line perform under production conditions? | run@rate, initial studies, MSA/SPC records, PPAP/PSW |
| Feedback and improvement | Does series production performance feed back into the system? | complaints, 8D, audits, lessons learned, FMEA/CP update |

PPAP is the submission of agreed-upon evidence to the customer, not an independent "standard folder." The submission level, the traditional 18 elements, initial capability criteria, and any threshold like Ppk/Cpk are defined by the applicable manual/CSR/agreement. Don't use `Ppk ≥ 1.67` as a universal rule.

### 3. AIAG-VDA FMEA, Control Plan, MSA, and SPC as a Single Flow

1. The flow diagram represents the actual process and its interfaces.
2. The PFMEA uses the seven steps: planning/preparation; structure analysis; function analysis; failure analysis; risk analysis; optimization; results documentation.
3. Prevention/detection controls and special characteristics flow down into the Control Plan, including method, sample, frequency, owner, and **reaction plan**.
4. Every measurement method used for decision-making is qualified through appropriate MSA; numerical GR&R, attribute, destructive, or nested, as the case requires.
5. SPC monitors stability and reaction; capability is only interpreted after verifying stability, distribution, rational subgrouping, and measurement adequacy.
6. PPAP demonstrates this coherence. A change in drawing, tooling, supplier, process, software, or measurement triggers a documented impact assessment and, where applicable, a review of every artifact and a submission to the customer.

**Golden rule:** no RPN/AP number, %GR&R, or Cpk substitutes for risk reasoning, customer requirements, and evidence that the reaction actually works.

### 4. Series Execution, Suppliers, and Failure Response

- **Special characteristics:** keep drawing/CSR identification consistent all the way through DFMEA, PFMEA, Control Plan, work instructions, data collection, reaction, and PPAP.
- **Suppliers:** qualification, scorecard, second-party audit, development, SCAR/8D, and effectiveness verification. Incoming quality doesn't eliminate responsibility for the purchased process.
- **8D:** protect the customer, describe the problem with data, contain, find and validate the root cause and escape point, implement corrective action, prevent recurrence, recognize the team. "Five whys" isn't proof of cause without validation.
- **LPA and CQI:** use layered process audits and special process assessments per scope, technology, customer, and the applicable manual edition.
- **Product safety and contingency:** define responsibilities, escalation, traceability, communication, contingency testing, and recovery; address embedded software and cybersecurity as applicable.

### 5. Integrating Case — Engineering Change

A customer changes a critical dimension and replaces an approved raw material. Before producing, build an impact matrix covering: CSR/contract; drawing and specification; feasibility; flow; DFMEA; PFMEA; special characteristics; Control Plan; MSA method/study; work instructions; training; capability/SPC; packaging/traceability; supplier; contingency plan; PPAP/PSW; and customer communication.

For each item, identify **owner, evidence, deadline, approval, and effectiveness criterion**. The goal isn't filling out documents: it's preserving safety, conformance, and capability under the change.

> **Study scope.** This chapter integrates automotive quality fundamentals with the chapters on suppliers, metrology, MSA, SPC, FMEA, and risk. The AIAG, AIAG&VDA, IATF, and VDA manuals and customer-specific requirements (CSRs) remain the applicable references for execution and certification.

### What's Missing, and Why It Matters

AIAG treats the **Core Tools** as an integrated system of six tools, not as six separate subjects:

$$\textbf{APQP} \;\rightarrow\; \textbf{FMEA} \;\rightarrow\; \textbf{Control Plan} \;\rightarrow\; \textbf{MSA} \;\rightarrow\; \textbf{SPC} \;\rightarrow\; \textbf{PPAP}$$

It's this chain that constitutes the competency sought in automotive recruiting — in markets with a strong component-supplier presence, it's frequently the eliminatory requirement.

| Missing Chapter | Minimum Necessary Content |
|---|---|
| **IATF 16949** | Additional requirements beyond ISO 9001; customer-specific requirements (CSR); product safety; contingency planning; embedded software; special processes |
| **APQP** | The five phases; deliverables per phase; responsibility matrix; phase reviews |
| **DFMEA / PFMEA (AIAG-VDA)** | The seven steps: planning, structure analysis, function analysis, failure analysis, risk analysis, optimization, documentation. Action Priority (AP) replacing RPN |
| **Control Plan** | Prototype, pre-launch, and production; special characteristics; measurement method; sample size and frequency; control method; **reaction plan** |
| **PPAP** | The 18 elements; the five submission levels; PSW; initial process studies ($P_{pk} \geq 1.67$) |
| **MSA (AIAG 4th edition)** | %GRR and ndc acceptance criteria; attribute studies; destructive testing |
| **Automotive SPC** | Subgroup, reaction, and capability requirements in a series-production context |
| **Special characteristics** | Identification, customer symbols, propagation DFMEA → PFMEA → Control Plan → PPAP |
| **Supplier quality** | Second-party audit, SCAR, formal 8D, scorecard, development |
| **Layered process audits (LPA)** and **CQI** | Special process assessments: heat treatment, coating, welding, molding, casting |

> **Terminology note.** The expression "six Core Tools" varies between organizations. The traditional AIAG list is generally presented as five tools (APQP, PPAP, FMEA, MSA, and SPC); the Control Plan is an essential APQP deliverable and may be taught separately. In addition, PPAP/capability criteria, including any minimum Ppk, depend on the current manual, CSR, and customer agreement.

### The Chain That Makes Sense of Everything Else

Automotive training's value isn't in knowing each document in isolation — it's in seeing that they're **a single information flow**:

1. The customer defines requirements and **special characteristics**.
2. **APQP** organizes the program into phases with deliverables.
3. **DFMEA** analyzes risk in the product design; **PFMEA** in the process.
4. PFMEA's prevention and detection controls flow down into the **Control Plan**.
5. The Control Plan's measurement methods have to be validated through **MSA**.
6. The characteristics monitored by **SPC** are the ones PFMEA identified as critical.
7. **PPAP** gathers the evidence that the process consistently produces conforming output.
8. Field problems come in through **8D** and feed back into the PFMEA and the Control Plan — closing the loop.

> **The test that separates those who know from those who memorized:** given an engineering change, which of these documents must be reviewed, in what order, and what gets submitted to the customer? Whoever can answer that has mastered the Core Tools.

### 2026 Update — AIAG Core Tools

In 2026, automotive training shouldn't treat the Control Plan as if it still sits only inside the old APQP. AIAG published **APQP 3rd Edition** in 2024 and **Control Plan 1st Edition** as a standalone document. The current Core Tools structure includes APQP, Control Plan, PPAP, AIAG & VDA FMEA, MSA, and the new **AIAG & VDA SPC Manual 1st edition**, published in 2026.

FMEA should be handled using the terminology and logic of the **AIAG & VDA FMEA Handbook**, including Action Priority where applicable, without indiscriminately mixing RPN from older approaches with the current AP.

**Customer rule:** in an automotive environment, the effective requirement can be determined by IATF 16949, the customer's/OEM's CSR, the contract, and the Core Tool manual edition the customer requires.
