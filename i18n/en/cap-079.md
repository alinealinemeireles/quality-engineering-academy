## Chapter 79: Taguchi, Robust Design, and Tolerance Design ✦

### The engineering question

*How do I build a process that stays good when real conditions drift away from ideal?*

Genichi Taguchi's contribution wasn't the statistics — it was the change in goal. Instead of asking "which values maximize the response?", you ask **"which values make the response insensitive to what I can't control?"**

That's the difference between optimizing and making robust, and it has large economic consequences.

### The quadratic loss function

The traditional quality model is binary: within tolerance = good, outside = bad. Taguchi rejects it as economically false.

$$L(y) = k(y - T)^2$$

The loss grows continuously with distance from the target $T$, even within spec. The constant $k$ is calibrated from the cost $A_0$ of exceeding tolerance $\Delta_0$:

$$k = \frac{A_0}{\Delta_0^2}$$

**A process's expected average loss:**

$$E[L] = k\left[\sigma^2 + (\mu - T)^2\right]$$

This expression is the bridge between the philosophy and the index: it's **exactly** $C_{pm}$'s denominator (PART VII). Two parts, both conforming, one at the target and the other at the edge of the limit, don't have the same value.

**The classic empirical argument:** Sony TVs manufactured in Japan and in the US both met the color-density specification. The Japanese units clustered around the target; the American ones were spread uniformly across the tolerance. The complaint rate was substantially different — because customers don't experience the specification, they experience the distance from the target.

### Control factors and noise factors

| | **Control** factors | **Noise** factors |
|---|---|---|
| Definition | The engineer chooses the level | Vary and aren't controllable in production |
| Examples | Geometry, material, process parameters | Ambient temperature, humidity, wear, lot, customer use |
| In the trial | Inner array | Outer array — **deliberately induced** |

**Taguchi's central idea is counterintuitive and brilliant:** to make a product robust to noise, **introduce the noise into the trial on purpose**. Vary the ambient temperature, use different lots, force wear. Then look for the combination of control factors whose response varies least in the face of that noise.

**The three types of signal-to-noise ratio:**

| Goal | S/N | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Nominal is best | $10\log_{10}\dfrac{\bar{y}^2}{s^2}$ | Dimensions, targets |
| Smaller is better | $-10\log_{10}\dfrac{1}{n}\sum y_i^2$ | Defects, wear, roughness |
| Larger is better | $-10\log_{10}\dfrac{1}{n}\sum \dfrac{1}{y_i^2}$ | Strength, yield, life |

**The statistical criticism — and it's serious.** The Western statistics community (Box, Hunter, and others) criticized this strongly: S/N ratios mix mean and variance into a single metric, making interpretation opaque; Taguchi's orthogonal arrays are frequently resolution III, confounding interactions; and Taguchi himself downplayed interactions that are often the most important thing.

**The modern synthesis, which is what's recommended:**

> Keep Taguchi's **philosophy** (robustness, induced noise, the loss function) and use Western **methodology**: model mean and variance **separately**, with combined control × noise designs and dual response modeling.

This gives you the best of both: the right goal, with defensible statistics.

### Worked example: parameter design in two approaches

```py-r
--- python
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
from itertools import product
import statsmodels.formula.api as smf
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

rng = np.random.default_rng(5150)

# Matriz INTERNA: 3 fatores de controlo (2^3)
controlo = pd.DataFrame(list(product([-1,1], repeat=3)), columns=["A","B","C"])
# Matriz EXTERNA: 2 fatores de ruído (2^2) — induzidos de propósito
ruido = pd.DataFrame(list(product([-1,1], repeat=2)), columns=["N1","N2"])

# Verdade: B interage com o ruído -> B controla a SENSIBILIDADE ao ruído
def resposta(A, B, C, N1, N2, r):
    return (100 + 6.0*A + 1.5*B + 3.0*C
            + (1.3 - 1.1*B)*N1                 # <-- B modula o efeito do ruído N1
            + (0.9 - 0.75*B)*N2
            + 0.8*A*C + r.normal(0, 0.6))

linhas = []
for _, c in controlo.iterrows():
    for _, n in ruido.iterrows():
        linhas.append({**c.to_dict(), **n.to_dict(),
                       "y": resposta(c.A, c.B, c.C, n.N1, n.N2, rng)})
d = pd.DataFrame(linhas)
print(f"Desenho cruzado: {len(controlo)} corridas de controlo × "
      f"{len(ruido)} condições de ruído = {len(d)} observações\n")

# ---------- ABORDAGEM 1: Taguchi clássico (S/N) ----------
res = d.groupby(["A","B","C"])["y"].agg(["mean", "std"]).reset_index()
res["SN_nominal"] = 10*np.log10(res["mean"]**2 / res["std"]**2)
res = res.round(3)
print("1) ABORDAGEM TAGUCHI — S/N 'nominal é melhor'")
print(res.to_string(index=False))

print("\n   Efeito de cada fator sobre o S/N (quanto maior, mais robusto):")
for f in "ABC":
    e = res.loc[res[f]==1,"SN_nominal"].mean() - res.loc[res[f]==-1,"SN_nominal"].mean()
    print(f"      {f}: {e:+.2f} dB")
melhor_sn = res.loc[res.SN_nominal.idxmax()]
print(f"\n   Melhor combinação por S/N: A={melhor_sn.A:+.0f}, B={melhor_sn.B:+.0f}, "
      f"C={melhor_sn.C:+.0f}  (S/N = {melhor_sn.SN_nominal:.2f} dB)")

# ---------- ABORDAGEM 2: modelação dual (recomendada) ----------
print("\n\n2) ABORDAGEM MODERNA — modelar média e dispersão SEPARADAMENTE")

# 2a. Modelo da MÉDIA, incluindo interações controlo × ruído
m_full = smf.ols("y ~ (A + B + C)*(N1 + N2)", data=d).fit()
inter = {k: v for k, v in m_full.params.items()
         if ":" in k and any(n in k for n in ["N1","N2"])}
print("   Interações CONTROLO × RUÍDO (é aqui que vive a robustez):")
for k, v in sorted(inter.items(), key=lambda kv: -abs(kv[1])):
    p = m_full.pvalues[k]
    print(f"      {k:<10} = {v:+.3f}  (p = {p:.4f})"
          f"{'   <-- significativo' if p < 0.05 else ''}")
print("\n   Uma interação controlo×ruído significativa significa que esse fator de")
print("   controlo ALTERA a sensibilidade ao ruído. É o mecanismo da robustez.")

# 2b. Modelo da MÉDIA e modelo do log da VARIÂNCIA
m_media = smf.ols("mean ~ A + B + C", data=res).fit()
res["log_var"] = np.log(res["std"]**2)
m_disp = smf.ols("log_var ~ A + B + C", data=res).fit()
print("\n   Modelo da MÉDIA:")
print("   " + m_media.params.round(3).to_string().replace("\n", "\n   "))
print("\n   Modelo da DISPERSÃO (log da variância):")
print("   " + m_disp.params.round(3).to_string().replace("\n", "\n   "))

# ---------- A estratégia em dois passos ----------
print("\n\n3) A ESTRATÉGIA DE ROBUST DESIGN EM DOIS PASSOS")
disp_ef = m_disp.params.drop("Intercept")
media_ef = m_media.params.drop("Intercept")
f_disp = disp_ef.abs().idxmax()
print(f"   Passo 1 — MINIMIZAR A VARIÂNCIA com os fatores que a afetam.")
print(f"      Fator dominante na dispersão: {f_disp} "
      f"(coef. {disp_ef[f_disp]:+.3f})")
nivel = -1 if disp_ef[f_disp] > 0 else 1
print(f"      Fixar {f_disp} = {nivel:+d} para minimizar a variância.")
candidatos = [f for f in "ABC" if abs(disp_ef[f]) < 0.3*abs(disp_ef[f_disp])]
if candidatos:
    f_aj = max(candidatos, key=lambda f: abs(media_ef[f]))
    print(f"\n   Passo 2 — AJUSTAR A MÉDIA ao alvo com um fator que NÃO afeta a variância.")
    print(f"      '{f_aj}' move a média ({media_ef[f_aj]:+.2f}) mas quase não afeta a")
    print(f"      dispersão ({disp_ef[f_aj]:+.3f}). É o 'fator de ajuste' ideal.")

ALVO = 100.0
melhor = None
for _, r in res.iterrows():
    if r[f_disp] != nivel: continue
    perda = r["std"]**2 + (r["mean"] - ALVO)**2      # função de perda de Taguchi
    if melhor is None or perda < melhor[0]:
        melhor = (perda, r)
print(f"\n   Combinação final: A={melhor[1].A:+.0f}, B={melhor[1].B:+.0f}, "
      f"C={melhor[1].C:+.0f}")
print(f"      média = {melhor[1]['mean']:.2f} (alvo {ALVO}) | "
      f"desvio-padrão = {melhor[1]['std']:.3f}")

# ---------- Comparação económica pela função de perda ----------
print("\n\n4) FUNÇÃO DE PERDA — o argumento económico")
A0, Delta0 = 60.0, 6.0                 # € de falha externa quando excede ±6
k = A0/Delta0**2
print(f"   k = A0/Δ0² = {A0}/{Delta0}² = {k:.2f} €/unidade²")
print("   Comparamos as combinações DEPOIS de a média ser ajustada ao alvo")
print("   (passo 2 da estratégia). A perda residual é então k·σ², ou seja,")
print("   é inteiramente devida à sensibilidade ao ruído.\n")
res["perda_unit"] = k*res["std"]**2
tabela = res[["A","B","C","mean","std","SN_nominal","perda_unit"]].sort_values("perda_unit")
tabela.columns = ["A","B","C","média","desvio-padrão","S/N (dB)","perda €/peça"]
print(tabela.round(3).to_string(index=False))
pior, mel = tabela["perda €/peça"].max(), tabela["perda €/peça"].min()
volume = 120_000
print(f"\n   Combinação mais robusta: {mel:.2f} €/peça (σ = {tabela.iloc[0]['desvio-padrão']:.2f})")
print(f"   Combinação menos robusta: {pior:.2f} €/peça "
      f"(σ = {tabela.iloc[-1]['desvio-padrão']:.2f})")
print(f"   Poupança a {volume:,} peças/ano = €{(pior-mel)*volume:,.0f}")
print("   -> e ambas as combinações produzem peças DENTRO da especificação,")
print("      com a mesma média. A diferença é só robustez ao ruído.")

fig, axes = plt.subplots(1, 2, figsize=(13, 4.5))
for b, cor, lab in [(-1, "#a5203a", "B = -1"), (1, "#31803a", "B = +1")]:
    sub = d[d.B == b]
    for n1 in [-1, 1]:
        s = sub[sub.N1 == n1]
        axes[0].scatter([n1]*len(s), s.y, color=cor, alpha=0.6, s=35)
    mm = sub.groupby("N1")["y"].mean()
    axes[0].plot(mm.index, mm.values, color=cor, lw=2, marker="o", label=lab)
axes[0].set_xlabel("fator de ruído N1"); axes[0].set_ylabel("resposta")
axes[0].legend(); axes[0].set_xticks([-1,1])
axes[0].set_title("Interação controlo × ruído\nB=+1 é MENOS sensível ao ruído (linha plana)")
ys = np.linspace(ALVO-8, ALVO+8, 300)
axes[1].plot(ys, k*(ys-ALVO)**2, lw=2)
axes[1].axvline(ALVO-Delta0, color="r", ls="--"); axes[1].axvline(ALVO+Delta0, color="r", ls="--")
axes[1].axvline(ALVO, color="g")
axes[1].set_xlabel("valor da característica"); axes[1].set_ylabel("perda (€)")
axes[1].set_title("Função de perda quadrática\n(a perda não é zero dentro da tolerância)")
plt.tight_layout(); plt.show()
```

**Engineering reading.** The chart on the left is the essence of robust design: with $B = +1$ the response is **flat** against the noise; with $B = -1$ it's tilted. Both levels give similar averages — the difference lies entirely in the **sensitivity**.

A traditional DOE focused only on the mean would have concluded B doesn't matter much. And it would have left the process's most valuable variable on the table.

The two-step strategy is what operationalizes this:

1. Use the factors that affect the **dispersion** to minimize it.
2. Use a factor that affects the **mean** but not the dispersion to hit the target.

This separation is possible precisely because factors rarely affect both things the same way.

### Tolerance Design: the last stage, and the most expensive

Taguchi organizes development into three stages, in mandatory order:

| Stage | What gets decided | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **1. System design** | Architecture, technology, concept | Low per unit |
| **2. Parameter design** | Robust nominal values | **Free** — it's a matter of choosing levels |
| **3. Tolerance design** | Tightening tolerances on critical components | **Expensive** — better material, more precise process, more inspection |

> **The rule:** only move to tolerance design after exhausting parameter design. Tightening tolerances is buying quality; choosing good nominals is getting it for free.

**Tolerance stack-up.** When an assembly characteristic results from several parts:

- **Worst case (arithmetic):** $T_{\text{assy}} = \sum |a_i| T_i$ — guarantees 100% interchangeability, but is very conservative and expensive.
- **Statistical (RSS):** $T_{\text{assy}} = \sqrt{\sum a_i^2 T_i^2}$ — assumes independence and centered distributions; much looser.

The difference between the two is often 40–50% in available tolerance, which translates directly into manufacturing cost.

```py-r
--- python
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd

# Montagem: folga = corpo - (eixo + anilha1 + anilha2)
comp = pd.DataFrame({
    "Componente": ["Corpo", "Eixo", "Anilha 1", "Anilha 2"],
    "Nominal":    [50.00, 30.00, 10.00, 9.50],
    "Tolerancia": [0.10, 0.06, 0.04, 0.04],       # ± valor
    "Coef":       [1, -1, -1, -1],
    "Custo_apertar_50pct": [1200, 3500, 400, 400],   # € /ano para reduzir a tolerância a metade
})
folga_nom = (comp.Coef*comp.Nominal).sum()

pior_caso = (comp.Coef.abs()*comp.Tolerancia).sum()
rss = np.sqrt(((comp.Coef**2)*(comp.Tolerancia**2)).sum())

print("EMPILHAMENTO DE TOLERÂNCIAS")
print(comp.to_string(index=False))
print(f"\nFolga nominal = {folga_nom:.3f} mm")
print(f"   Pior caso (aritmético) : ± {pior_caso:.4f} mm")
print(f"   Estatístico (RSS)      : ± {rss:.4f} mm")
print(f"   O RSS liberta {(1-rss/pior_caso):.0%} de tolerância face ao pior caso.\n")

REQUISITO = 0.14      # a montagem exige folga dentro de ± 0,14 mm
print(f"Requisito da montagem: ± {REQUISITO} mm")
print(f"   Pior caso  : {'CUMPRE' if pior_caso <= REQUISITO else 'NÃO CUMPRE'} "
      f"({pior_caso:.4f})")
print(f"   RSS        : {'CUMPRE' if rss <= REQUISITO else 'NÃO CUMPRE'} ({rss:.4f})")

# --- Contribuição de cada componente para a variância da montagem ---
comp["Contrib_var"] = (comp.Coef**2)*(comp.Tolerancia**2)
comp["% da variância"] = (comp.Contrib_var/comp.Contrib_var.sum()*100).round(1)
comp["Ganho se apertar 50%"] = [
    round(rss - np.sqrt((((comp.Coef**2)*(comp.Tolerancia.where(
        comp.index != i, comp.Tolerancia/2)**2)).sum())), 5)
    for i in comp.index]
comp["€ por 0,001 mm ganho"] = (comp.Custo_apertar_50pct /
                                 (comp["Ganho se apertar 50%"]*1000)).round(0)
print("\nONDE INVESTIR — análise de sensibilidade económica")
print(comp[["Componente", "Tolerancia", "% da variância",
            "Ganho se apertar 50%", "Custo_apertar_50pct",
            "€ por 0,001 mm ganho"]].to_string(index=False))

melhor = comp.loc[comp["€ por 0,001 mm ganho"].idxmin()]
print(f"\n=> Apertar '{melhor.Componente}' é a opção mais eficiente: "
      f"€{melhor['€ por 0,001 mm ganho']:.0f} por cada 0,001 mm de tolerância ganha.")
print("   Note que NÃO é necessariamente o componente que mais contribui para a")
print("   variância — é o melhor rácio entre contribuição e custo de o melhorar.")

# --- Verificação por simulação de Monte Carlo ---
rng = np.random.default_rng(1)
N = 200_000
# assumindo Cp = 1,33 em cada componente (tolerância = 4 sigma)
sim = np.zeros(N)
for _, r in comp.iterrows():
    sigma = r.Tolerancia/4
    sim += r.Coef*rng.normal(r.Nominal, sigma, N)
print(f"\nSIMULAÇÃO DE MONTE CARLO (Cp = 1,33 em cada componente, {N:,} montagens)")
print(f"   Folga: média = {sim.mean():.4f} | desvio-padrão = {sim.std():.5f}")
print(f"   Fora de ± {REQUISITO} mm: "
      f"{np.mean(np.abs(sim - folga_nom) > REQUISITO)*1e6:,.0f} ppm")
print(f"   (o cálculo RSS assume componentes centrados; a simulação permite")
print(f"    testar o efeito de descentramentos e de distribuições não-normais)")
```

### Excel and Power BI (DAX)

Taguchi's signal-to-noise (S/N) ratio is, by far, this block of chapters' most Excel-friendly metric — it's a simple logarithmic formula over each run's replicates, exactly how it was calculated manually before DOE software became widespread:

```py-r
--- excel
' S/N "quanto maior melhor" (ex.: resistencia, rendimento):
=-10*LOG10(MEDIA(1/(Replicas_range^2)))
' S/N "quanto menor melhor" (ex.: defeitos, tempo de ciclo):
=-10*LOG10(MEDIA(Replicas_range^2))
' S/N "nominal e melhor" (ex.: uma dimensao com alvo definido):
=10*LOG10(MEDIA(Replicas_range)^2/VAR.P(Replicas_range))
--- dax
Razao SN Nominal e Melhor :=
VAR media_resp = AVERAGE ( Repeticoes[Valor] )
VAR var_resp = VAR.P ( Repeticoes[Valor] )
RETURN 10 * LOG10 ( DIVIDE ( media_resp ^ 2, var_resp ) )
```

These three formulas (larger-is-better, smaller-is-better, nominal-is-best) cover the three types of quality characteristic Taguchi's method handles and can be calculated directly in a runs table in Excel — one of the few cases, among Chapters 51-56 and 74-79, where the original statistical technique was historically designed for manual spreadsheet calculation, and Excel reproduces that workflow without loss.

### Common mistakes

1. **Using S/N without understanding what it mixes together.** Model mean and variance separately; it's more informative and defensible.
2. **Using resolution-III orthogonal L arrays without checking aliases.** Taguchi's arrays are fractional factorials under another name, and the same confounding rules apply.
3. **Not including noise factors in the trial.** Without induced noise there's no information about robustness, and "robust design" is just a regular DOE.
4. **Moving to tolerance design before exhausting parameter design.** You end up buying quality you could have had for free.
5. **Applying RSS without checking the assumptions.** RSS assumes independent, centered components with similar distributions. If a supplier systematically produces off-center parts, the formula underestimates the assembly's variation. Simulate.
6. **Ignoring the loss function in the economic argument.** It's the tool that translates variation into euros, and it's what makes management understand why hitting the target is worth it.
